Calculation method & limitations — Read more

Monthly reducing-balance model: annual nominal interest ÷ 12. EMI and monthly interest are rounded to paise; the final EMI is adjusted to clear the balance. Rate changes use the outstanding principal. Events after payoff are ignored; excess part-payments are capped at the balance. “Keep EMI” may shorten or lengthen the loan. If EMI cannot cover interest, no misleading schedule is shown.

This is for illustrative and educational purposes only, not a lender quotation, approval or financial advice. Actual dates, daily-interest methods, rate-reset terms, fees, taxes, insurance and prepayment rules may differ. Only fees you enter are included; no fee exemption is assumed. Comparison cost is interest plus entered fees, not APR. Discuss the lender’s actual schedule and terms before deciding.

Input tenure is limited to 480 months. Scenarios exceeding 1,200 months are not projected. The calculator does not model moratoriums, irregular instalments or disbursement tranches.

Useful questions
Can I enter 12.5 years?

Yes. It equals 150 monthly instalments. You can also switch the input to whole months.

Should I reduce EMI or tenure after a part-payment?

Compare both scenarios. Keeping EMI generally repays the balance earlier; keeping the remaining tenure usually lowers EMI. Your cash-flow needs and lender terms matter.

Can I model a rate increase and part-payment together?

Yes. Add both events and choose their month and effect. Rate changes happen before the EMI; part-payments happen after it.
